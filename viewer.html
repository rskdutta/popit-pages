Viewer perception

Know what viewers feel before they scroll.

A panel of viewers — inside your audience and deliberately a few outside it — watches your reel. They react at the second it happens, rate it, say whether they would buy, and write the comment they would leave.

Try it on a reel

Reactions on your timeline

Every reaction lands on the second it was felt, so you see the moment a viewer was won or lost, not an average.

  • Love, want, lost, bored — tapped at the moment, by each viewer.
  • Who stayed to the end and where most left.
  • Would they buy, skip or save? — intent, not a star rating.
  • What they would write — the comments, grouped into themes.

What you get

📍

Moments, not averages

The second a viewer leaned in and the second they left. Each fix points at a time stamp you can jump to.

🧭

Your audience and the edge of it

A quarter of the panel sits outside your declared audience on purpose, so you also learn who else your reel reaches.

💬

The comments before the comments

What people would write, grouped: praise, confusion, complaints, questions — with the exact words.

📈

Scores that move

Hook, clarity, pull, fit and buy intent as bands, with how far each moves if you swap the viewers. Honest ranges, not false precision.

Real viewers take over.

The panel starts simulated so you can see results on day one. As real viewers pop your reel, their reactions replace the panel's — the picture only gets truer.
