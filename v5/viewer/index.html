01 · Viewer perception

Know what viewers feel before they scroll

A panel of viewers — inside your audience and deliberately a few outside it — watches your reel. They react at the second it happens, rate it, say whether they would buy, and write the comment they would leave.

What you get

  • Moments, not averages — The second a viewer leaned in and the second they left. Each fix points at a time stamp you can jump to.
  • Your audience and the edge of it — A quarter of the panel sits outside your declared audience on purpose, so you also learn who else your reel reaches.
  • The comments before the comments — What people would write, grouped: praise, confusion, complaints, questions — with the exact words.
  • Scores that move — Hook, clarity, pull, fit and buy intent as bands, with how far each moves if you swap the viewers. Honest ranges, not false precision.
At 0:04

“Took too long to get to the point, but the ending sold me.”

Most of the viewers who left did so in the opening. The ones who stayed past 0:04 rated the ending highest — the fix is at the start, not the end.

Real viewers take over

The panel starts simulated so you can see results on day one. As real viewers pop your reel, their reactions replace the panel's — the picture only gets truer.
